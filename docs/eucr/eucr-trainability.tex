\title{EUCR: An Evidential Continual Learner, and an Accounting of
Which of Its Parts Do the Work}
\author{EUCR design note}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
EUCR is the evidential analogue of EWC: a Dempster--Shafer (DS) prototype head on
a 1D ResNet, with per-stage evidential probes whose uncertainty supplies a
MAS-style consolidation importance. It works as a continual learner --- on a
four-task IQ radar sequence it reaches a final F1 of $0.596$ against EWC's
$0.427$, though it trails Learning-without-Forgetting's $0.660$ --- but reaching
that number required fixing two measurement faults and one numerical fault, and
the accounting of \emph{which} of its parts produce it is largely negative.

Three findings carry the note. First, EUCR was being scored on a different
evaluation protocol from every model it was compared against: every
\texttt{ResNet1D}-based learner reaches its backbone through a \texttt{forward}
whose \texttt{bn\_training} defaults to \texttt{True}, so the benchmark uses
\emph{batch} normalisation statistics, while EUCR --- lacking that wrapper ---
used frozen running statistics. That asymmetry alone was worth $0.31$ final F1.
Second, the consolidation anchor's loss form is outside its stability window at
every $\lambda$ tested: $\Omega$ is normalised to unit mean but has median
$2.7\times10^{-4}$ and maximum $1.7\times10^{4}$, so a few tail coordinates make
the anchor gradient outweigh the task gradient $60\times$ and the global
gradient clip then attenuates the task signal to $1.6\%$ of its size. A
closed-form proximal anchor removes this, worth a further $+0.07$ final F1 and a
$14\times$ reduction in seed variance. Third, and least comfortably for the
method's premise: comparing importance \emph{frontiers} rather than points, a
fixed random linear readout on the same stage features traces the same
trade-off curve as the evidential one, reparameterised by a constant. The
per-coordinate weighting contributes ($+0.017$ over uniform $\Omega$); nothing
about it needs to be learned, and nothing about it needs to be evidential.

We also record the closed-form Dempster fusion that makes the head affordable
(40--90$\times$ on that layer, $6.6\times$ end-to-end, outputs matching the
sequential loop to $2\times10^{-6}$), a construction-level pathology in which the
ignorance mass is identically zero, a pre-registered gate in which neither the
ignorance nor the conflict readout beats a softmax baseline at novelty
detection, and a set of measured claims that the code previously asserted
without evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Structure of the model}

\subsection{Where the code lives}

Four modules, in dependency order:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{model/evidential\_modules.py} --- the Dempster--Shafer
        primitives: \texttt{Distance\_layer},
        \texttt{DistanceActivation\_layer}, \texttt{Belief\_layer},
        \texttt{Omega\_layer}, \texttt{Dempster\_layer},
        \texttt{DempsterNormalize\_layer}, assembled into
        \texttt{Dempster\_Shafer\_module}; plus the two readouts (\texttt{DM},
        \texttt{pignistic\_probability}) and the two losses
        (\texttt{EvidentialLoss}, \texttt{PignisticNLLLoss}).
  \item \texttt{model/eucr\_backbone.py} --- \texttt{EucrResNet1D}, a ResNet-18
        style 1D backbone whose linear \texttt{fc} head is replaced by per-task
        DS heads, with an \texttt{EvidentialProbe} hanging off each of the four
        residual stages.
  \item \texttt{model/eucr\_consolidation.py} --- importance estimation
        (\texttt{compute\_importance}), channel pooling (\texttt{to\_channel}),
        cross-task accumulation (\texttt{accumulate}), the weight anchor
        (\texttt{snapshot}) and the quadratic \texttt{penalty}.
  \item \texttt{model/eucr.py} --- the learner \texttt{Net} that the La-MAML
        harness drives: \texttt{observe} for a training step,
        \texttt{finalize\_task\_after\_training} for the end-of-task
        consolidation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data and backbone}

Inputs are 1024-sample flat IQ vectors, reshaped to two channels
$(2,512)$ (I and Q), min--max scaled per feature using training-split
statistics. The backbone is the standard ResNet-18 arrangement in 1D:
\texttt{conv1}/BatchNorm/ReLU/maxpool, then four stages of two
\texttt{BasicBlock1D} each at widths $64,128,256,512$, global average pooling,
and a \texttt{LayerNorm} on the pooled 512-d feature. Dropout ($p=0.2$) sits
between stages.

An important property of the data: roughly half of every task's samples carry
the label $-1$, meaning \emph{noise} rather than any emitter class (48\% for
\texttt{t2-rcn}, exactly 50\% for the \texttt{deeprad} files). The task-incremental
loader remaps each task's labels into a contiguous block and, in the default
(non-collapsed) mode, appends noise as the \emph{last} class of that block:
\texttt{neg\_label = labels\_offset + unique\_labels.size}. Evaluation macro
recall then excludes that class. This matters for reading results: a model that
predicts the noise class for every sample scores exactly $0.000$, because every
counted class has zero recall. The alarming zeros in the June logs are that
degenerate state, not a numerical failure.

\subsection{The Dempster--Shafer head}

A DS module maps a feature vector $f \in \mathbb{R}^{F}$ to a mass function over
the singleton classes plus the frame $\Theta$ (``ignorance''), i.e.\ a vector in
$\mathbb{R}^{C+1}$. It does so through $P$ prototypes, $P = C \cdot
\texttt{proto\_factor}$:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Distance.} $d_p = 1 - \cos(f, w_p)$ for each prototype $w_p$
        (cosine is the default; the pooled features are \texttt{LayerNorm}'d, so
        their norm is near-constant and squared-Euclidean distance is dominated
        by that constant).
  \item \textbf{Activation.} $s_p = \alpha_p \exp(-\gamma_p d_p)$ with
        $\gamma_p=\eta_p^2$ and $\alpha_p=\sigma(\xi_p)$, followed by a division
        by $\max_p s_p$.
  \item \textbf{Belief.} Prototype $p$ splits its activation over classes with
        weights $u_{cp} = \beta_{cp}^2 / \sum_{c'}\beta_{c'p}^2$, giving a mass
        function $m_p(c) = u_{cp}s_p$ and $\omega_p = 1 - s_p$.
  \item \textbf{Fusion.} The $P$ prototype mass functions are combined by
        Dempster's rule and normalised.
\end{enumerate}

For singleton-plus-ignorance masses, Dempster's rule for two sources is
\begin{equation}
m(c) \;\propto\; m_1(c)m_2(c) + m_1(c)\omega_2 + \omega_1 m_2(c),
\qquad
\omega \;\propto\; \omega_1\omega_2 .
\label{eq:dempster}
\end{equation}

Two readouts sit on top. \texttt{DM} produces per-class expected utilities
$m(c) + (1-\nu)\omega$ with $\nu\omega$ retained in the last column, trained by
\texttt{EvidentialLoss} (per-class BCE plus a gated KL warm-up).
\texttt{pignistic\_probability} produces the Smets transform
$\mathrm{BetP}_c = m(c) + \omega/C$, a genuine distribution, trained by
\texttt{PignisticNLLLoss}. The task-incremental configuration uses the pignistic
head.

\subsection{Per-task heads, shared probes}

\texttt{EucrResNet1D} owns one DS head per task, each over only that task's class
count, and routes \texttt{forward(x, t)} through head $t$, scattering its scores
into the global logit vector at the task's offsets. A head is trained only
during its own task and receives no gradient afterwards, so it is frozen by
construction; the shared backbone is what consolidation has to protect.

The four stage \emph{probes} are the opposite: they are shared across tasks and
built at the \emph{global} class width $n_{\text{outputs}}$, and their loss is
not masked to the current task. Each probe average-pools its stage feature map,
\texttt{LayerNorm}s it, and runs its own DS module. Their combined loss enters
the objective at weight \texttt{probe\_loss\_weight}.

\subsection{Consolidation}

After task $t$, \texttt{compute\_importance} differentiates the mean backbone DS
uncertainty --- selectable as nonspecificity ($\omega$), discord
($H(\mathrm{BetP})/\log C$), or their sum --- with respect to every shared
backbone parameter, accumulating $\lVert\nabla\rVert^2$ over the task's data.
The result is optionally pooled to one score per convolution output channel,
normalised to unit mean, and summed across tasks. During task $t+1$ the loss
gains $\lambda\sum_i \Omega_i(\theta_i - \theta_i^{\star})^2$. No pruning, no
masks. Despite the shape of the formula this is \emph{not} a Fisher
approximation: there is no Laplace / log-likelihood-curvature story behind it.
Squaring the gradient of a scalar \emph{output functional} over effectively
unlabelled data is Memory Aware Synapses (Aljundi et al., ECCV 2018,
$\nabla\lVert F(x)\rVert^2$), with a DS uncertainty substituted for MAS's output
norm. It should be described as a MAS variant and benchmarked against MAS.

\subsection{The training step}

\texttt{Net.observe} runs one forward pass with the probes, forms
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathcal{L}_{\text{head}}
\;+\; w_{\text{probe}}\,\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\text{probe}}}
\;+\; \lambda \, \mathcal{R}_{\text{consolidation}},
\end{equation}
and steps two parameter groups separately: the shared backbone at \texttt{lr},
and the evidential parameters (heads, probes, and the distance-activation
$\eta,\xi$) at a fraction of it. Each group is gradient-clipped against its own
budget so the high-gradient head cannot scale the backbone's update to nothing.
Mixed precision is disabled in three independent places --- \texttt{amp: false}
in the config, a hard override in \texttt{main.py}, and an
\texttt{autocast(enabled=False)} guard inside the step --- on the stated grounds
that the DS head's exponential and normalisation chains are unstable in reduced
precision. Section~\ref{sec:amp} tests that claim.


\subsection{Knobs added during this study}

Five options were added while establishing the results below. All default to the
previous behaviour except where stated.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{--eucr\_anchor\_mode \{loss, proximal\}} --- the anchor's form
        (Section~\ref{sec:anchor}). Default \texttt{loss}, but \texttt{proximal}
        is the recommended setting and the shipped config now uses it.
  \item \texttt{--eucr\_bn\_stats \{batch, running, freeze, per\_task\}} ---
        the normalisation-statistics protocol (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
        \textbf{Default changed} to \texttt{batch}, which matches the harness.
  \item \texttt{--eucr\_uncertainty \{...,\ uniform, random\_proj\}} --- two
        ablation controls on the importance readout (Section~\ref{sec:frontier}).
  \item \texttt{--eucr\_belief\_init \{random, class\}} --- round-robin
        prototype-to-class initialisation of $\beta$.
  \item \texttt{--eucr\_ce\_aux\_weight}, \texttt{--eucr\_ds\_detach} --- an
        auxiliary per-task linear cross-entropy head sharing the backbone, and
        an option to stop the DS head's gradient at the pooled feature.
  \item \texttt{--eucr\_temper}, \texttt{--eucr\_activation\_norm},
        \texttt{--eucr\_readout\_scale} --- the cautious-combination family used
        to revive the ignorance mass in Section~\ref{sec:gate}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Two measurement faults and one numerical fault}

\subsection{EUCR was scored on a different protocol from its baselines}
\label{sec:protocol}

\texttt{ResNet1D.forward} carries a \texttt{bn\_training} parameter defaulting to
\texttt{True}, and its body is

\begin{verbatim}
prev = self.model.training
self.model.train(bn_training)
try:    ...
finally: self.model.train(prev)
\end{verbatim}

so it forces its inner module into training mode on \emph{every} call. Verified
directly: after \texttt{model.eval()}, a forward still reports
\texttt{bn1.training == True}. Every \texttt{ResNet1D}-based learner in the
repository --- EWC, SI, ER, GEM, LwF --- therefore evaluates with \emph{batch}
normalisation statistics, transductively. No evaluation path passes
\texttt{bn\_training=False}; the 21 call sites that do are importance passes,
replay encoding and distillation inside \texttt{woe\_si}. This is a known and
deliberate property: the \texttt{--eval-bn} help text in
\texttt{scripts/confusion\_from\_run.py} already records that ``every reported
number in this project uses batch statistics''.

\texttt{EucrResNet1D} had no such wrapper, so EUCR inherited whatever
\texttt{model.eval()} set --- frozen running statistics carried over from the
last task trained. It was the only model in the table on that protocol. Since
consecutive tasks are different radar datasets, the two protocols differ a great
deal. Re-scoring existing checkpoints, with no retraining (three seeds):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
 & \multicolumn{3}{c}{EUCR} & \multicolumn{3}{c}{EWC} \\
\cmidrule(lr){2-4}\cmidrule(lr){5-7}
protocol & diag & final & BWT & diag & final & BWT \\
\midrule
\texttt{batch} (harness) & $0.645$ & $\mathbf{0.600}$ & $-0.060$ & $0.713$ & $0.428$ & $-0.380$ \\
\texttt{running} (frozen) & $0.616$ & $0.293$ & $-0.432$ & $0.678$ & $0.339$ & $-0.452$ \\
per-task buffers          & $0.616$ & $0.365$ & $-0.335$ & $0.678$ & $0.198$ & $-0.639$ \\
AdaBN recalibration       & $0.647$ & $0.599$ & $-0.063$ & $0.681$ & $0.434$ & $-0.330$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For EWC the protocol barely matters ($0.428$ against $0.434$); for EUCR it is
worth $0.31$ final F1 and $0.37$ BWT. \texttt{EucrResNet1D.forward} and
\texttt{Net.forward} now take \texttt{bn\_training}, and
\texttt{--eucr\_bn\_stats} defaults to \texttt{batch}.

Two caveats belong with this. Lifting task $j$'s \emph{stored} buffers is not a
valid per-task protocol --- they were estimated under task $j$'s weights, so
pairing them with later weights describes a different network, which is why that
row is the worst for EWC. AdaBN recalibration (re-estimating statistics on task
$j$'s data with the current weights, forward passes only) is the correct version,
but it requires retaining data from each past task, which is a replay assumption
a regularisation-only method rejects. And \texttt{batch} is transductive: a
sample's prediction depends on the others in its evaluation batch. Swept over
test batch sizes $32/64/128/512$, the effect is real but mild (task-0 recovery
$0.731/0.742/0.748/0.769$ on one seed). There is no option here that is both
non-transductive and storage-free.

\subsection{The anchor's loss form is numerically unstable}
\label{sec:anchor}

Explicit descent on $\lambda\,\Omega\,(\theta-\theta^\star)^2$ has curvature
$k = 2\lambda\Omega$ and is stable only while $\mathrm{lr}\cdot k < 2$.
\texttt{compute\_importance} normalises $\Omega$ to unit \emph{mean}, but the
distribution is extremely heavy tailed, so the mean is set entirely by the tail:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
 & p50 & p90 & p99 & p99.9 & p99.99 & max \\
\midrule
$\Omega$ & $2.7\times10^{-4}$ & $3.5\times10^{-3}$ & $0.42$ & $23.7$ & $1809$ & $1.7\times10^{4}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The median is $3600\times$ below the mean. At the shipped $\lambda=0.09$ and
$\mathrm{lr}=0.01$, 490 coordinates sit outside the stability window with
$\mathrm{lr}\cdot k = 30.7$ at the maximum. Measured on a batch perturbed
$0.01$ from $\theta^\star$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
$\lambda$ & $\lVert g_{\text{task}}\rVert$ & $\lVert g_{\text{anchor}}\rVert$ & ratio & clip factor \\
\midrule
$0.09$ & $5.13$ & $311$              & $61$              & $0.0158$ \\
$1$    & $5.24$ & $4.6\times10^{3}$  & $886$             & $1.1\times10^{-3}$ \\
$10$   & $5.00$ & $5.8\times10^{4}$  & $1.2\times10^{4}$ & $8.6\times10^{-5}$ \\
$1000$ & $4.59$ & $6.5\times10^{6}$  & $1.4\times10^{6}$ & $7.7\times10^{-7}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Because \texttt{clip\_grad\_norm\_} rescales by one global scalar, a handful of
tail coordinates drag the whole network's effective learning rate down: at the
shipped $\lambda$ the task gradient reaches the optimiser at $1.6\%$ of its size.
This explains a result that otherwise makes no sense --- raising $\lambda$ made
\emph{both} diagonal and final F1 worse, rather than trading one for the other.

The fix is the proximal (backward-Euler) form, which evaluates the anchor
gradient at the new point and solves in closed form to a convex combination:
\begin{equation}
b = 2\,\mathrm{lr}\,\lambda\,\Omega,
\qquad
\theta_{\text{new}} = \frac{\theta^{+} + b\,\theta^\star}{1+b}.
\label{eq:prox}
\end{equation}
The mixing weight $b/(1+b)$ saturates at $1$ for any $\Omega$, so the update can
never overshoot. Applied after the optimiser step, with the penalty removed from
the backward pass entirely.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
 & diagonal F1 & final F1 & BWT \\
\midrule
no anchor                  & $0.7173 \pm 0.0068$ & $0.3261 \pm 0.0193$ & $-0.391$ \\
loss form, $\lambda=0.09$  & $0.6265 \pm 0.0145$ & $0.5231 \pm 0.0533$ & $-0.103$ \\
proximal, $\lambda=500$    & $0.6317 \pm 0.0058$ & $0.5695 \pm 0.0179$ & $-0.062$ \\
proximal, $\lambda=5000$   & $0.6019 \pm 0.0042$ & $\mathbf{0.5932 \pm 0.0038}$ & $-0.009$ \\
proximal, $\lambda=50000$  & $0.5674 \pm 0.0032$ & $0.5679 \pm 0.0028$ & $+0.001$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Three things change at once. Final F1 rises $+0.070$; the $\lambda$ sweep becomes
\emph{monotone}, tracing a proper stability--plasticity frontier instead of
degrading at both ends; and the seed spread falls $14\times$, from $\pm0.0533$ to
$\pm0.0038$. That last point retires a claim made repeatedly in earlier drafts of
this note --- that EUCR's large seed variance was a coherent property of
prototype-based evidential heads. It was substantially this bug. Note that
proximal's useful $\lambda$ is far larger than the loss form's, because $b$
carries a factor of $\mathrm{lr}$: unit $\Omega$ gives $b=1$ at
$\lambda = 1/(2\,\mathrm{lr}) = 50$.

\section{What the consolidation signal actually is}
\label{sec:frontier}

With a stable anchor the question ``does the evidential weighting contribute
anything'' becomes answerable for the first time. It could not be asked under the
loss form, because the clip factor depends on $\lVert g_{\text{anchor}}\rVert$
and hence on $\Omega$'s tail --- a uniform $\Omega$ has no tail, so the two arms
were being attenuated by different amounts and the comparison was confounded.

Four importance sources were swept over $\lambda$ and compared as
\emph{frontiers}, because matched $\lambda$ is not matched pull when the $\Omega$
distributions differ:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{evidential} --- the shipped readout.
  \item \textbf{uniform} --- $\Omega_i = 1$ everywhere, i.e.\ plain L2-SP.
  \item \textbf{frozen probes} --- probes never trained (\texttt{probe\_loss\_weight 0}
        gives them no gradient at all; verified, drift $=0.0000$ across the
        sequence), still read through the DS fusion.
  \item \textbf{random projection} --- MAS with a random head: the gradient of
        $\lVert Wf_s\rVert^2$ for a fixed random Gaussian $W$ on the same pooled,
        normalised stage features. Everything the probes do is kept except the DS
        fusion and its learned parameters.
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
importance source & peak final F1 & at diagonal \\
\midrule
random projection & $\mathbf{0.5962 \pm 0.0015}$ & $0.604$ \\
evidential        & $0.5932 \pm 0.0038$ & $0.602$ \\
frozen probes     & $0.5901 \pm 0.0065$ & $0.596$ \\
uniform $\Omega$  & $0.5791 \pm 0.0011$ & $0.577$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The evidential and random-projection frontiers are not merely close at their
peaks: they are \emph{the same curve, shifted one decade in $\lambda$}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
position on frontier & evidential (diag / final) & random proj.\ (diag / final) \\
\midrule
high plasticity & $\lambda{=}5$: $0.697 / 0.390$ & $\lambda{=}0.5$: $0.703 / 0.386$ \\
mid             & $\lambda{=}500$: $0.632 / 0.570$ & $\lambda{=}50$: $0.641 / 0.569$ \\
optimum         & $\lambda{=}5000$: $0.602 / 0.593$ & $\lambda{=}500$: $0.604 / 0.596$ \\
saturated       & $\lambda{=}50000$: $0.567 / 0.568$ & $\lambda{=}5000$: $0.566 / 0.567$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every paired row matches to within $\pm0.01$ on both axes. The decade offset is
predicted by the $\Omega$ statistics: the random readout's median $\Omega$ is
$57\times$ the evidential median, so the same pull $b = 2\,\mathrm{lr}\,\lambda\,\Omega$
arrives at roughly $1/57$ the $\lambda$. And this is not a relabelling: the two
$\Omega$ vectors have Spearman correlation $0.546$, so about half the coordinate
ranking differs --- yet the frontier is identical.

The conclusion has three parts, and only the first is positive.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The anchor does the work.} $+0.15$ final F1 over no anchor at all.
  \item \textbf{Per-coordinate weighting adds a little.} $+0.017$ over uniform
        $\Omega$ ($0.5962$ against $0.5791$, $\sigma \approx 0.001$--$0.002$;
        separable).
  \item \textbf{None of that weighting need be learned, and none of it need be
        evidential.} Evidential, frozen-DS and random-projection are mutually
        indistinguishable (within $\pm0.003$ against $\sigma \approx
        0.002$--$0.007$). $\Omega$ is a layer-and-channel sensitivity measure that
        any random readout recovers.
\end{enumerate}

This agrees with the per-coordinate result in Section~\ref{sec:pre1}, where a
displacement control beat every importance measure on a restoration test.

\section{The evidential content}

\subsection{The fusion admits a closed form}

\texttt{Dempster\_layer} applied Dempster's rule in a Python loop, fusing
prototypes one at a time: $P-1$ sequential iterations with $P$ as large as $138$.
Adding the ignorance column to a class column gives the commonality
$q_k(c) = m_k(c) + \omega_k$, and the rule is then exactly
$q(c) = q_1(c)q_2(c)$ alongside $\omega = \omega_1\omega_2$. Both are products,
so fusing $P$ sources is two reductions:
\begin{equation}
\log q(c) = \sum_{p} \log\!\big(m_p(c) + \omega_p\big),
\qquad
\log \omega = \sum_{p} \log \omega_p,
\qquad
m(c) = q(c) - \omega .
\label{eq:closedform}
\end{equation}
The loop's per-step renormalisation is a positive per-sample scalar the recursion
carries through linearly, so it only rescales the result --- this is not an
approximation. Outputs agree with the loop to $2\times10^{-6}$ and gradients to
$5\times10^{-13}$; the layer is $9.6\times$ faster at $P=18$, $46\times$ at
$P=81$ and $78\times$ at $P=138$, and an epoch falls from $8.9$\,s to $1.35$\,s,
matching EWC's per-epoch cost. Working in logs with a max-shift also removes the
underflow that $P$-fold products invite.

\subsection{The ignorance mass is zero by construction}

The measured $\omega$ is $\sim10^{-16}$ at the head and $10^{-14}$ to $10^{-25}$
at the probes. Three independent constructions cause this, not one.
(i) \texttt{DistanceActivation\_layer} ends with
$s \leftarrow s/(\max_p s_p + 10^{-4})$, pinning the best prototype at
$s\approx1$ and its ignorance at the $4.2\times10^{-4}$ rounding artefact.
(ii) The $P$-fold product $\omega = \prod_p(1-s_p)$.
(iii) Cosine distance is bounded in $[0,2]$, so with $\gamma\approx1$ and
$\alpha=\sigma(0)=0.5$ activations live in $[0.068, 0.5]$ and no input, however
novel, can drive $s_p \to 0$. Denoeux's ENN relies on unbounded Euclidean
distance precisely so novel inputs send mass to $\Theta$.

Measurement makes (iii) worse than the bound suggests. After training,
$\gamma \in [0.95, 1.08]$ and $\alpha \in [0.498, 0.502]$ --- essentially
unmoved from initialisation --- and this is \emph{not} gradient starvation:
gradient norms on $\eta$ and $\xi$ ($1.9$--$5.6\times10^{-2}$,
$1.1$--$2.0\times10^{-2}$) are the same order as on the prototypes and belief
weights ($4.8$--$7.2\times10^{-2}$, $3.6$--$7.5\times10^{-2}$). The layer gets a
normal gradient and does not move. Observed cosine distances span only
$[0.60, 1.34]$, so no prototype ever approaches a feature and
$\prod_p(1-s_p)$ is nearly constant in the input.

Two consequences follow, both verified. \texttt{eucr\_uncertainty=both} is
\emph{bit-identical} to \texttt{discord} ($\max|\text{both}-\text{discord}| = 0$),
so that flag has two live settings, not three. And with $\omega\approx0$,
$\mathrm{BetP}_c = q(c) - \omega(C{-}1)/C$ reduces to $q(c)$, so by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:closedform} the normalised output is \emph{exactly}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BetP} \;=\; \mathrm{softmax}\Big(\textstyle\sum_p \log\big(1 - s_p(1-u_{cp})\big)\Big),
\label{eq:softmax}
\end{equation}
measured to $\max|\mathrm{BetP}-\mathrm{softmax}| = 1.8\times10^{-7}$ with argmax
agreement $1.0000$. In its shipped configuration the DS head \emph{is} a softmax
classifier over an unusual, saturating, non-negatively-weighted log-linear score.

An important distinction, since an earlier draft of this note got it wrong:
$\nabla\omega \propto \omega$, so although $\omega$'s \emph{value} is
$\sim10^{-14}$, its relative sensitivity \emph{pattern} is well defined and
survives the unit-mean normalisation. \texttt{nonspecificity} is dead as a
\emph{value} (novelty detection) but alive as an \emph{importance pattern}, and
performs the same as \texttt{discord} in that role.

\subsection{A pre-registered gate: does the formalism earn its place?}
\label{sec:gate}

Given Eq.~\eqref{eq:softmax}, EUCR has a claim to be more than a softmax only if
the $\omega$ and $K$ columns can be revived \emph{and} then carry information a
softmax does not. Pre-registered as a gate; both halves came back negative.

\paragraph{Revival works.} Three changes together: drop the max-division
(\texttt{--eucr\_activation\_norm none}); temper the fusion by dividing both
log-sums by $P^{\tau}$ (\texttt{--eucr\_temper}), the geometric mean of
commonalities at $\tau=1$, which stops $P$ prototypes reading \emph{one} feature
vector being counted as $P$ independent sources; and rescale the decision logits
by $P^{\tau}$ (\texttt{--eucr\_readout\_scale untemper}). Median $\omega$ rises
from $10^{-20}$ to $0.78$. The third change is essential rather than cosmetic:
tempering shrinks the readout logits by $P^\tau$, and without undoing that on the
decision, $\tau=1$ trains to macro recall $0.000$ against $0.710$ with it.
Tempering appeared untrainable; it was only cold. $q_p(c)\ge\omega_p$ survives
geometric means, so $m(c)\ge0$ holds for any $\tau$.

\paragraph{A confound had to be removed first.} Every task is $\sim$50\% noise,
and although the in-distribution set excluded noise \emph{rows}, the head still
had a noise \emph{output} that absorbs anything unfamiliar. Noise-prediction
rates on cross-file OOD were $0.04/0.64/0.76$ across seeds against
$0.02/0.11/0.18$ in-distribution. Retraining with noise dropped entirely roughly
doubles every score on the held-out-class test and \emph{annihilates} the
cross-file column (all scores $0.46$--$0.56$, the noise floor). Cross-file
novelty was never being detected; the noise class was.

\paragraph{It buys nothing.} Held-out-class novelty (train on three of
\texttt{t2-rcn}'s signal classes, score the other two), noise-free, signed AUROC,
three seeds at matched accuracy:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
score & held-out-class & $|{\cdot}-0.5|$ \\
\midrule
$\log\omega$ (DS)   & $0.274\pm0.035$ & $0.226$ \\
$\log K$ (DS)        & $0.721\pm0.044$ & $0.221$ \\
max BetP (softmax)   & $0.230\pm0.027$ & $\mathbf{0.270}$ \\
entropy (softmax)    & $0.227\pm0.025$ & $\mathbf{0.273}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The softmax baselines are the strongest scores on the table. Under a true null
the $|\mathrm{AUROC}-0.5|$ statistic has expectation $\approx 0.8\sigma$, so for
the observed per-seed $\sigma$ the floor is $0.02$--$0.12$. Signed AUROC is
reported because almost every score is \emph{anti}-correlated with novelty:
unseen emitters are classified \emph{more} confidently and with \emph{less}
ignorance than seen ones. $\log K$ is the only correctly-signed score --- its
magnitude ties rather than beats, but a score thresholdable in the natural
direction is operationally worth more than an equally strong one that must be
inverted. That is the one thread of the formalism that survives, and it is thin.

\paragraph{And it costs accuracy.} On the four-task benchmark, tempering with
$\omega$ revived gives final F1 $0.329$ (\texttt{nonspecificity}) and $0.386$
(\texttt{discord}) against $0.51$--$0.53$ with $\omega$ dead. The configuration in
which the evidential quantity is meaningful is the worst-performing one.

\subsection{PR-E1: a displacement control beats every importance measure}
\label{sec:pre1}

Pre-registered. On task-0 held-out data at end of sequence, restore the top-$k\%$
of shared backbone coordinates by a measure's rank to their task-0 values and
measure the fraction of the loss recovered, relative to a
statistics-restored baseline. At the primary $k=1\%$, three seeds:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
measure & $R(1\%)$ \\
\midrule
$|\Delta\theta|$ \textbf{(control)} & $\mathbf{+0.699 \pm 0.104}$ \\
Fisher $\times\,\Delta\theta^2$     & $+0.682 \pm 0.161$ \\
MAS-entropy $\times\,\Delta\theta^2$ & $+0.579 \pm 0.020$ \\
Fisher                              & $+0.515 \pm 0.073$ \\
MAS-entropy (shipped)               & $+0.334 \pm 0.222$ \\
$|\Delta\theta \cdot g|$            & $+0.318 \pm 0.248$ \\
$|\theta_0^\star|$ (control)        & $+0.103 \pm 0.152$ \\
random (control)                    & $+0.006 \pm 0.007$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The gate passes ($R_{\text{MAS}} - R_{\text{random}} \ge 0.10$ in 3/3 seeds,
sanity kill not triggered), but it was the wrong comparison: forgetting is
localisable --- 1\% of coordinates restores 70\% of it --- yet the coordinates
are identified by \emph{how far they moved}, not by any importance functional.
Multiplying either importance by $\Delta\theta^2$ moves it most of the way to
$|\Delta\theta|$ and neither product beats it, so the $\Delta\theta^2$ factor
carries the signal. One caveat that cuts against the headline: $R(k)$ rewards L2
proximity to $\theta_0^\star$, which $|\Delta\theta|$ maximises by construction,
so the products rather than the control are the fair test --- and they say the
importance term buys nothing.

\section{Other measured properties}

\subsection{Mixed precision: lossy, not unstable}
\label{sec:amp}

The comment justifying the \texttt{autocast(enabled=False)} guard asserted the
head is ``unstable in AMP''. Neutralising the guard and training for real:
\textbf{zero} non-finite losses and zero skipped steps across nine four-task runs
and three single-task runs, in both bf16 and fp16. Paired per-seed diagonal F1
against fp32: bf16 $-0.032 \pm 0.031$ (not significant), fp16
$-0.085 \pm 0.012$ (significant, but these runs use no \texttt{GradScaler}, so an
unknown share is missing loss scaling). AMP buys $\sim$5\% epoch time
($6.19$\,s $\to$ $5.89$\,s), because after the Dempster fix the model is dominated
by small kernel launches rather than matmul FLOPs. \textbf{Keep the guard, for the
cost/benefit reason, not the stability one.} Note that layer-level precision
diagnostics rank the dtypes the \emph{opposite} way from the end-to-end result
(bf16 gradient cosine to fp32 is $0.32$--$0.50$ against fp16's $0.976$--$0.998$),
so they did not predict end-to-end damage here.

\subsection{Two architectural choices that are load-bearing}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Euclidean distance destroys the model}: diagonal and final F1
        both $0.0974 \pm 0.0001$ with BWT exactly $0.000$ on all three seeds --- it
        never learns. The pooled features are \texttt{LayerNorm}'d, so their L2
        norm is near-constant and squared-Euclidean distance is dominated by that
        constant. Cosine is not a tuning choice.
  \item \textbf{GroupNorm destroys the model}: $0.0981 \pm 0.0001$, training
        accuracy $0.00$, loss pinned at $2.23$. BatchNorm's batch statistics are
        load-bearing for the cosine-prototype head.
\end{itemize}

\section{Positioning the method}

\subsection{What EUCR is, stated honestly}

An evidential prototype head with per-task DS heads and a MAS-style importance
anchor. On this benchmark it is a working continual learner that beats EWC
comfortably and loses to LwF. Its retention is excellent and its plasticity is
its weakness --- diagonal $0.60$ against $0.71$ for both baselines.

The parts that demonstrably contribute are: the quadratic anchor ($+0.15$),
applied in proximal form ($+0.07$ on top, plus a $14\times$ variance reduction),
with \emph{some} per-coordinate weighting ($+0.017$), and per-task heads that are
frozen by construction. The parts that do not contribute, on measurement, are the
Dempster--Shafer fusion as a source of that weighting, the deep evidential
supervision on the probes, and both uncertainty readouts as novelty signals.

That is a narrower method than the original framing, but it is a defensible one,
and the accounting is more useful to a reader than the claim would have been.

\subsection{Report the evaluation protocol}

The single most consequential number in this note was produced by neither the
model nor the data but by which normalisation statistics the evaluation used.
Any paper using this harness should state that its numbers are computed with
batch statistics, and therefore transductively, and should report at least one
alternative. The three options are set out in Section~\ref{sec:protocol}; none is
both non-transductive and storage-free.

\subsection{Calibrate $\lambda$ against the stability window, not by search}

$\Omega$'s heavy tail means the effective anchor strength is set by a few
coordinates. Under the loss form, check $\max_i \mathrm{lr} \cdot 2\lambda\Omega_i
< 2$ before trusting any $\lambda$; under the proximal form this is unnecessary by
construction, which is the main argument for preferring it. Reporting the ratio
$\lVert g_{\text{anchor}}\rVert / \lVert g_{\text{task}}\rVert$ alongside
$\lambda$ would have made this visible immediately.

\subsection{Compare importance schemes as frontiers}

Matched $\lambda$ is not matched pull when the $\Omega$ distributions differ ---
uniform $\Omega$'s median is $3600\times$ the evidential median. Every
point-comparison of importance schemes in earlier drafts of this note was
therefore uninterpretable. Sweep $\lambda$ and compare the (diagonal, final)
trade-off curves.

\subsection{Where the remaining plasticity is}

The detached CE arm reaches diagonal $0.744$, so the representation the DS head
needs is reachable; the head simply does not shape it well on its own. That, plus
the $\gamma/\alpha$ layer never training despite receiving normal gradients,
localises the plasticity deficit in the distance-to-activation parameterisation
rather than in the evidential formalism. It is the most promising remaining
direction and the one this note does not settle.

\section{Reproducing}

\begin{verbatim}
main.py --config configs/base.yaml --config configs/models/til/eucr.yaml \
  --task-order-files "t2-rcn,t0-rcn,t0-deeprad,t1-deeprad" \
  --samples_per_task 10000 --n_epochs 10 --expt_name <arm>
\end{verbatim}

About two minutes per arm; several fit on one A6000 concurrently. Every
invocation is a three-seed sweep --- read all three per-seed
\texttt{results.txt}, never seed 0 alone. Decision rules and amendments for the
pre-registered gates are in \texttt{docs/eucr/preregistration.md}.
